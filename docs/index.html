Why this research?

The problem, the objective and what the research delivers

🚚

The problem

Olist is a marketplace: every seller ships directly to the customer. 76% of items leave São Paulo state, but only 42% of orders go there, so the average parcel travels ~775 km and takes 12 days.

🎯

The objective

Design a fulfilment-centre (FC) model end to end, and quantify its trade-offs in service, cost and capital, to support a real go / no-go decision.

❓

The main question

Should Olist run its own FC network, and if so, how? It splits into three linked decisions: where (network), how (delivery) and what (stock).

📦

Outputs

A recommended FC network · daily routes and fleet mix · a stock list with reorder points · forecast comparison · trade-off curves · reproducible Python + SQL.

👥

Who it is for

Supply-chain and operations leaders deciding on network, fleet and inventory, and product managers building fulfilment services for sellers.

Overall conclusion

✅
An FC network is a speed strategy, not a cost-saving one. FCs cut distance in half and raise next-day reach to 58%, but they save only ~R$ 3 of freight per item while inventory costs ~R$ 17 per unit. Recommendation: start with a São Paulo FC holding only the fast-moving products, deliver with a mixed fleet, and fund it through seller fulfilment fees (the Amazon FBA model). Add Rio and the Northeast as volume grows.
