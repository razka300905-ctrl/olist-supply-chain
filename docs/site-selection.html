1. Purpose

  • Question: if Olist moves from "every seller ships" to its own fulfilment centres (FCs), how many should it open, and where?
  • Objective: find the number and location of FCs that bring orders closest to customers, and show what each extra FC is worth.
  • Output: a recommended network, a 1–6 FC trade-off curve, the KPIs per scenario, and a map of which FC serves which region.

2. The answer

Open 3 FCs: São Paulo, the Minas Gerais/Rio corridor, and the Northeast. In practice that means São Paulo + Rio de Janeiro + Salvador.

3. Key findings

  1. 3 FCs is the knee of the curve. The 3rd FC lifts next-day reach from 39% to 58%, the biggest jump of any step. After 5 FCs the curve is flat.
  2. Big metros cost almost nothing extra. The model's exact sites (Juiz de Fora, Lagarto) are only 4% better on distance than São Paulo + Rio + Salvador, which are far easier to staff and supply.
  3. FCs are about speed, not freight. Each extra FC saves only R$ 80k → 39k → 30k → 27k → 14k of freight a year. The business case is faster delivery.
  4. Supply is concentrated, demand is not. 76% of items ship from São Paulo state, but only 42% of orders go there. That mismatch is why parcels travel ~775 km today.

4. How we got there: steps and decisions

Step 1 · Clean the data. We used valid delivered orders only, with one median coordinate per zip code. Why: the raw files have duplicated map points, coordinates outside Brazil and orders that never shipped.

Step 2 · Group demand into regions. Orders were grouped into 849 three-digit zip regions, weighted by number of orders. Why: 5-digit zips (15k points) are too many to solve exactly, and 27 states are too coarse. One order is one delivery, so orders, not revenue, measure the service need.

Step 3 · Choose candidate sites. The top 3 regions per state plus the top 60 nationally, 119 candidates in total. Why: every region gets a candidate, and the model stays small enough (~100k variables) for a proven optimum.

Step 4 · Choose the model. A p-median model, solved exactly with PuLP + HiGHS for k = 1…6. It opens k sites and minimises order-weighted road distance. Why: it is the standard model for "open k sites, serve demand as close as possible". Alternatives considered: centre of gravity (handles one site only), k-means (no proven optimum), maximal coverage (needs an arbitrary radius).

Step 5 · Measure distance. Straight-line distance × 1.3. Why: transparent and free. 1.3 is a common road-detour factor, and it is accurate enough to compare scenarios.

Step 6 · Turn km into business KPIs. Two regressions on ~110k real shipments: delivery days ≈ 8.6 + 0.47 per 100 km, and freight ≈ R$ 8.2 + 0.82 per 100 km + 2.58 per kg. Why: managers decide on days and Reais, not kilometres.

Step 7 · Pick the network. 3 FCs (the knee), then swap the mathematical sites for the nearest big metros (+4% distance).

5. Risks: what would change the decision

  • Volume grows ~3×: freight savings scale up, and 4–5 FCs start to pay for themselves.
  • Inbound cost to the Northeast is high: supply sits in São Paulo, so the 3rd FC may shift to Rio/Belo Horizonte.
  • Real FC costs: an FC is worth opening only if it costs less than its freight saving plus the value of faster delivery.

6. Next step

The São Paulo FC serves ~62% of orders. Project 2 plans its daily delivery routes, and Project 3 decides what it should stock.

A

Method

  1. Clean data: valid delivered orders only; one median coordinate per zip code (project 0).
  2. Demand regions (SQL): orders grouped into 849 three-digit zip regions. Candidates: the top 3 regions per state + the top 60 nationally (119).
  3. p-median model: open exactly k sites, assign every region to one, and minimise order-weighted road distance (straight line × 1.3). Solved to proven optimality with PuLP + HiGHS for k = 1…6.
  4. From km to KPIs: regressions on ~110k real shipments. Delivery days ≈ 8.6 + 0.47 per 100 km (R² 0.18). Freight ≈ R$ 8.2 + 0.82 per 100 km + 2.58 per kg (R² 0.53).

Assumptions & limitations

  • Outbound only: restocking FCs from sellers is not costed (76% of supply is already in São Paulo).
  • No FC fixed costs: they are not in the data, so the break-even cost per FC is shown instead.
  • Estimated KPIs are directional (R² 0.18 for days): use them to compare scenarios, not to promise an SLA.
  • No capacity limits; historical demand (2017–18).
