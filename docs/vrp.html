Where the orders go

The service area is the whole metro region, not only São Paulo city

1. Purpose

  • Question: every morning the São Paulo FC must deliver yesterday's orders. How many vehicles, which type, at what cost, and what happens on a peak day?
  • Objective: find the cheapest fleet and routes that deliver every order within one shift, on a normal day and on Black Friday.
  • Output: optimised routes per vehicle, the fleet size and mix, the cost per order, and a peak-day capacity gap.

2. The answer

Run a mixed fleet of vans and motorbikes, and contract extra riders for peak days.

KPI Normal day (63 orders) Black Friday (248 orders)
Best fleet 2 vans + 2 motorbikes 6 vans + 9 motorbikes
Cost per order R$ 23.34 R$ 19.69
Saving vs a greedy "nearest next stop" plan −28% −38%
Orders late with only the normal-day fleet – 130 of 248 (52%)

3. Key findings

  1. A mixed fleet wins on both days. Vans carry the bulky parcels, and motorbikes do the many small ones at a third of the cost per km.
  2. Motorbike-only looks cheap but fails service. 4–6 orders a day are too bulky for a 90-litre box.
  3. Vans are limited by time, not space. They run ~10% full but use the whole 9-hour shift, so stops per hour matter more than capacity.
  4. Peaks need a plan. Black Friday has 4× the orders, and the normal fleet would leave half of them a day late.
  5. Density lowers cost. With 4× the orders, cost per order falls (R$ 23.34 → R$ 19.69) because stops are closer together.

4. How we got there: steps and decisions

Step 1 · Define the area. The whole Grande São Paulo metro region (zip 01000–09999). São Paulo city is ~70% of orders; the rest comes from 10–22 neighbouring municipalities such as São Bernardo do Campo, Guarulhos, Osasco and Santo André. Why: last-mile delivery is daily round trips from one depot. Leaving out the neighbouring cities would understate the fleet, and destinations 500+ km away go by line-haul truck instead.

Step 2 · Use project 1's depot. The São Paulo FC from the recommended network. Why: it serves ~62% of national orders, and it links "where" (project 1) to "how" (this project).

Step 3 · Pick real days. Thu 1 Mar 2018 (63 orders, closest to the median Tue–Thu of Mar–May 2018) and Black Friday, 24 Nov 2017 (248 orders, the busiest day). Why: real days keep the real mix of locations and parcel sizes, and the peak tests whether the plan holds.

Step 4 · Build the orders. One stop per order at its zip-code centroid, with weight and volume summed from product dimensions. Why: the data has no street addresses, and zip level is accurate to a neighbourhood.

Step 5 · Choose the model. A capacitated vehicle routing problem: weight and volume limits, a 9-hour shift, a mixed fleet. The objective is the lowest cost (cost per vehicle-day + cost per km). Why: it uses every constraint we have data or a sound assumption for. Customer time windows are not in the data.

Step 6 · Solve it. Google OR-Tools with guided local search (20–60 s). The mixed fleet starts from the van-only plan, so it can only match or beat it. Why: an exact MILP is too slow for 248 stops, and OR-Tools is free and industry-standard.

Step 7 · Compare against reality-like baselines. A greedy "nearest next stop" dispatcher, and Black Friday with only the normal-day fleet. Why: this shows what optimisation is worth (−28% to −38%) and turns "it's busy" into a number (130 late orders).

Assumptions (illustrative): van 650 kg / 3,300 L, 22 km/h, R$ 1.10/km + R$ 380/day. Motorbike 20 kg / 90 L, 30 km/h, R$ 0.35/km + R$ 180/day.

5. Risks: what would change the decision

  • Customer delivery time windows: vans lose flexibility first, which favours a bigger motorbike share.
  • Cheaper vans (e.g. electric): van-only gets closer to the mixed fleet.
  • Real road distances and traffic: more vehicles are needed, but the ranking of the fleets likely stays the same.

Method

Model. A capacitated vehicle routing problem with a 9-hour shift limit and a mixed fleet. Every order is visited once; every vehicle respects its weight, volume and time limits. The objective is to minimise (cost per vehicle-day) + (cost per km), solved with Google OR-Tools (guided local search). The mixed-fleet search starts from the van-only plan, so it can only match or beat it.

Data. Valid delivered orders for customers in Grande São Paulo (zip 01000–09999), with weight and volume from the product data.
